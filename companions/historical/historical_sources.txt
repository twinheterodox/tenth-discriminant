HISTORICAL DEPENDENCIES: DOCUMENTATION FOR THE 1830 DRAFT
Prepared 21 October 2026

Claim and interpretation
------------------------
The note gives a mathematical proof and proposes a historical cutoff of
1830. These are separate claims. Its interpretation of Tao's restriction
on "results and concepts" allows new constructions and deductions using
objects, operations and results documented by 1830. An elementary definition
or proof alone does not establish the historical eligibility of a concept.

In particular, the note treats the finite array of polynomial derivatives
as a construction from determinants, polynomials, derivatives and quadratic
congruences. Its properties are established directly, without a general
theorem about interpolation determinants. The historical judgement is
whether this is an admissible new use of old concepts. The foundation
dates below provide evidence for that judgement; correctness of the
mathematical proof alone does not decide it.

The note does not claim that this construction or proof was known in 1830,
or that an earlier year is impossible. References H1-H4 document historical
foundations; M1-M8 give modern attribution and context. In-context footnotes
headed "Modern attribution" identify contemporary sources of the argument.
Every mathematical assertion taken from a modern source and needed in the
proof is derived in the paper. This file is not an independent audit verdict.

1. Gauss, Disquisitiones arithmeticae (1801)
-----------------------------------------
Original edition: ETH-Bibliothek Zurich, Rar 5186.
https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-4025
https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/1254642?lang=en
Searchable OCR of the original (some OCR errors):
https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/download/ftpack/plain/1254642

Section V begins at printed page 165, Article 153, with integral binary
quadratic forms in Gauss's ax^2+2bxy+cy^2 convention. Articles 154 onward
treat representation and the determinant b^2-ac. Articles 157-159 treat
integral substitutions and proper/improper equivalence. Articles 171-175
treat reduction, finite enumeration, and equivalence classes.

The note uses Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2 and D=B^2-4AC. For odd B, doubling the whole
form produces Gauss's integral triple (2A,B,2C); for even B use (A,B/2,C).
Scaling preserves proper equivalence. The modern word "fundamental" is
used only for the explicit squarefreeness/congruence restriction given
in the paper. No later theorem relating forms to ideals is needed.

The small-prime obstruction is proved directly by displaying a primitive
form that represents p and invoking the hypothesis that there is only
one proper class. The proof neither assumes nor proves ideal factorization.
The nine surviving discriminants are checked by a reduction argument
given in full, followed by a search with leading coefficient at most 7.

2. Cauchy, Cours d'analyse (1821)
--------------------------------
Text of the 1882 collected-works reprint (its title page gives 1821):
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Cours_d%E2%80%99analyse_de_l%E2%80%99%C3%A9cole_royale_polytechnique
Relevant determinant chapter:
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Cours_d%E2%80%99analyse_de_l%E2%80%99%C3%A9cole_royale_polytechnique/Chapitre_III

Chapter III, section 2, constructs the alternating determinant expression
by replacing exponents in a Vandermonde expansion with indices, discusses
its alternating property, and applies it to general linear equations.
Chapter IV treats polynomial interpolation. Chapters VI-VIII document
series, complex expressions and their moduli, and complex polynomial
functions. The note needs only permutation expansion, elimination, the
triangle inequality, and elementary polynomial identities.

Hadamard's inequality has been removed. The substitute is
|det M| <= N! times the product of rowwise entry bounds, with N! <= N^N.
This raises the determinant error from 4 log R/R to 6 log R/R. The final
cutoff is still 32768, as checked in the note and verify_constants.py.

3. Cauchy, Resume des lecons ... sur le calcul infinitesimal (1823)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Original edition: ETH-Bibliothek Zurich, Rar 10369.
https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-25962
https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/8350182
Original OCR:
https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/download/ftpack/plain/8350182

Lessons 3-10 treat derivatives, partial derivatives and differentiation.
Lessons 21-25 treat definite integrals, their evaluation and geometric
interpretation. These support the integral comparisons, polynomial
derivatives, elementary monotonicity and logarithmic bounds used here.
The partial-summation identity in the note is explicitly derived by
summing f(p)=f(X)-integral_p^X f'(t) dt over finitely many primes.
The logarithm and arctangent estimates are also derived in the paper.

4. Galois, Sur la theorie des nombres (June 1830)
------------------------------------------------
Bulletin des sciences mathematiques, physiques et chimiques 13 (1830),
428-435; reprinted in Oeuvres mathematiques (1897), pages 15-23.
https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_math%C3%A9matiques/5

The text introduces expressions a+a_1 i+...+a_(nu-1)i^(nu-1), where i
satisfies an irreducible congruence modulo p, counts the p^nu expressions,
and treats their arithmetic. It derives alpha^(p^nu)=alpha and describes
the p-power conjugates. It explicitly uses the derivative of a polynomial
congruence to discuss repeated roots. The first source footnote identifies
the original publication as volume XIII, page 428, June 1830. Thus 1897
is the reprint date, not the date assigned to these concepts.

Our note uses only degree two, explains inverses by the polynomial
Euclidean algorithm, and proves z^p=conjugate(z). Polynomial root counts
and the derivative/multiplicity statement are also explained. No modern
classification of finite fields, Galois theory, or ideal quotient theorem
is invoked. The name "Frobenius" is modern shorthand for the displayed
p-power congruence already present in Galois's 1830 treatment.

5. Prime estimates and finite calculation
-----------------------------------------
There is no appeal to a published Chebyshev, Mertens, or prime number
theorem. The prime estimates are new deductions here from binomial
coefficients, factorization of factorials, and integral comparison.
Counting multiples proves the factorial valuation identity directly.
The final exclusion is a 30-row CRT table containing 104 witnesses, plus
15 witnesses below 164. The CRT step is explained by Bezout's identity.
No electronic or mechanical computation is a premise of the proof.
The optional code checks the displayed tables and constants exactly.
